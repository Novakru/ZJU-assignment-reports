\noindent\textbf{Outcome distribution and whether humans always won}\par\noindent Neither side won every game. The human won games 6 and 8; the agent won the other recorded games. Human performance differs from the synthetic opponents' zero win rate: success against fixed policies does not establish the same advantage against people.\par\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Critical moves, strengths, and mistakes}\par\noindent Games 1, 2, 6, and 8 cover both outcomes. Enumerating every legal penultimate move shows that all four human choices in game 1 allowed an immediate agent win. In game 2, four of five choices avoided immediate defeat, but the played move did not. In games 6 and 8, none of the agent's five and three choices, respectively, could prevent the human's next-move win. Those defeats were already unavoidable at the penultimate move.\par\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation weaknesses and improvements}\par\noindent Depth-three search and immediate safety ratios do not guarantee long-term routing safety. The losses expose weaknesses, although final boards cannot identify an individual weight's causal effect. Compare longer-route and threat features at the same depth and validate against new automatic opponents.\par\medskip
